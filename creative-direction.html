A

Creative Direction

Ten decisions. Everything downstream obeys them.

01

One film, eleven scenes — not a page with sections

The visitor holds the razor. Section boundaries are edits from the brand book's own vocabulary: cut to black, aperture iris, whip pan, freeze frame + text. Four transition types, used with reason. A fade is what you use when you haven't decided what the cut means.

02

Darkness is the default state. Light is earned.

#0A0A0A is not a background colour — it is the cinema screen before the projector fires. Every colour shift on the site is one of the five approved pairings from Section 07, assigned to a scene like a lighting plan. There is no sixth combination. No gradients anywhere, ever.

03

One strong element per frame

The photography rule becomes the layout rule. If two things compete for the eye on one screen, one gets removed. This is the hardest constraint in the build and the single thing that keeps it from drifting into agency-template territory.

04

Type is a graphic object, not text

Two sizes exist: enormous and 10px. Display runs past the viewport edge, sits off-centre, set tight at −3% tracking. Labels sit at +30% tracking, quiet in the corners. Nothing lives in between. That gap is the layout system — District's off-balance poster logic, built into the grid.

05

The work opens before the copy

Reel at scene 02. Work at scene 05. Explanation is the second act. The portfolio is the argument — STOM's reel-first logic, and the brand book's own website spec: nothing competes with the work.

06

Microdrama is a place, not a service

Its own colour, its own aspect ratio, its own scroll physics, its own route. Entering it should feel like leaving a website and opening a channel. It is the one owned category — it cannot sit in a row of three cards.

07

Production furniture, quietly

A running timecode in one corner. A scene number in the other. A 3px Crimson rule as the only divider on the site. It reads as a slate, not as UI. Beyond that there is no chrome — no cards, no shadows, no borders that aren't structural.

08

The held shot

At four moments the scroll resists and the frame holds one beat longer than expected. Straight from Section 11: that extra beat is where the feeling lands. Used four times. Not five — the fifth would make it a mannerism.

09

90% discipline, 10% bawaal — and the 10% is pre-allocated

Five moments, counted: the aperture cold open · the whip pan into POST · the Crimson manifesto slam · the vertical microdrama object · the day-mode inversion inside a case study. Anything else that asks to be unexpected gets cut. Chaos with intent means someone is counting.

10

It has to be beautiful with zero assets

The placeholder state is designed as a real state — frame outlines, grain, a slate label, a visible filename. On day one with no client films the site still reads as a studio holding its frames back, never as a broken template. Assets drop in later without a redesign.

Note on references

DNEG, STOM, Red Bull, Cash App, District and A24 are used exactly as the brand book intends — as design intelligence. Nothing is lifted. The one place a reference becomes literal is the Crimson manifesto screen, and that's because your own Story Template B already specifies it: full-bleed Crimson, white typography only, zero images — the colour IS the message.

Scene By Scene

What you see, what happens, what it costs in seconds.

Scene 00

Cold Open

Black · 3.2s · skippable
  • Black. 400ms of nothing. Not a spinner, not a progress bar, not a percentage. A real cold open has the nerve to make you wait.
  • One point of Crimson light, 6px, dead centre. It breathes once — the dot of light in the aperture, Section 05.
  • The aperture opens around it: the B-mark's counter form scaled to the viewport, clip-path circle, 900ms on the cut easing. Grain rises 0 → 10% as it opens.
  • Through the aperture: the reel, already running, already mid-frame. In medias res — never over-set-up.
  • Wordmark resolves at 1.4s. Holds 600ms. Then the statement slams in, second line 80ms behind the first:
    WE DON'T MAKE ADS. / WE MAKE BAWAAL.
  • Cut to black. 120ms. You are at the hero.
  • Skippable on any input. Runs once per session; returning visitors get a 600ms iris only. Reduced motion gets a 400ms crossfade with no iris and static grain.
  • Sound stays off. A 10px SOUND ON toggle sits bottom-right. Audio never autoplays — it's an accessibility failure and a trust failure in the same gesture.
Cut to black — 120ms
Scene 01

Hero

100vh · reel-dominant
  • Full-viewport reel, muted, looped, Bawaal Signature Grade baked in, 10% grain over it.
  • Type sits on the lower third, left-anchored — never centred. Display XL, −3.5% tracking, optically bleeding 0.04em past the left margin so it feels printed rather than placed.
  • Bottom right: PRODUCTION · POST · MICRODRAMAS at 10px, and a live timecode 00:00:14:08 ticking against the reel.
  • No scroll chevron. No "scroll to explore." A bouncing arrow is the most template-shaped object on the internet. The running timecode is the invitation — something is playing, and you want to see more of it.
Scene 02

Showreel

Pinned · held shot ①
  • The hero doesn't scroll away — it contracts. The full-bleed video insets to a 16:9 frame with a 12vw margin of pure black. Frame within a frame: it literally becomes a cinema screen inside a dark room.
  • Around it, two labels and nothing else: SHOWREEL / 2026 top-left of the frame, 01 / 07 bottom-right.
  • Scroll inside the pin scrubs the reel through seven chapters. Each chapter title appears bottom-left for 800ms, then leaves. You learn the studio's range without reading a services list.
  • Cursor over the frame becomes a Crimson dot reading PLAY. Click unmutes and expands back to full bleed.
  • Held shot: the pin holds one extra beat at chapter 07 before releasing.
Cut to black — 240ms hold, label only: 01 / PRODUCTION
Scene 03A

Production

100vh · full-bleed
  • Full-bleed production footage — cameras, sets, monitors, crew, real BTS. Motivated light, hard sources, practicals in frame.
  • Type: FROM FIRST IDEA / TO FINAL FRAME. Display XL, breaking the right edge of the viewport.
  • The capability list is not cards. It is one line of 10px labels along the bottom edge, like a slate strip:
    Brand Films · TVCs · Commercials · YouTube · Campaign Films · Shoot Production
    Reads in one second. Occupies no attention. Cards would have cost the whole scene.
Whip pan — 260ms, horizontal, motion blur present
Scene 03B

Post

Navy + Gold · pinned · 4 states
  • The only whip pan on the site lands here. Everything goes Midnight Navy — the register changes, and you feel it before you read anything.
  • One frame is held and scroll drives four states on that same frame:
  • ① RAW — flat, ungraded, log-flat and lifeless.
  • ② GRADE — the Bawaal Signature Grade lands over 700ms. The six parameters print themselves into the left margin in Gold, 10px, as they apply: lift · gamma · gain · sat −10 · grain 10% · S-curve.
  • ③ CUT — the frame hard-cuts to the next shot. One frame. No transition. That's the point.
  • ④ SOUND — a waveform draws in Gold along the bottom edge, and the next frame's motion starts three frames before its visual cut. Sound leads picture, Section 11, implemented literally.
  • Copy: THE STORY CONTINUES / AFTER THE SHOOT.
  • This is the one place technical precision is the flex — and the reason it doesn't become a software demo is that you never see a UI. You only see the film changing.
Slam cut — to full-bleed Crimson

Crimson · 600ms

Scene 04

Microdrama Experience

Signature · held shot ②

Scene 05

Selected Work

Largest scene · 100vh per project
  • No grid. A vertical sequence of full-bleed frames, one viewport each, alternating format so the rhythm never settles:
  • P01 16:9 full-bleed video · P02 4:5 still right-aligned at 62% width, enormous negative space left carrying the type · P03 9:16 vertical centred in black void · P04 full-bleed video, edge to edge, type overlaid low.
  • Metadata lives at 10px bottom-left, always present at 40% opacity. On hover it goes to 100%, the title slams in at Display M, and the image scales 1.00 → 1.04 over 800ms. Images breathe slower than type — Section 09, exactly.
  • Cursor becomes VIEW.
  • Filtering is an edit, not a filter. A 10px strip rotated 90° along the left edge: ALL / BRAND FILMS / TVCS / MICRODRAMAS / YOUTUBE / POST. Selecting one doesn't fade and reflow — non-matching frames are gone in one frame. It reads editorial, never e-commerce.

Scene 06

How We Make Bawaal

Amber · pinned horizontal

Scene 08

Manifesto

Crimson + White only · held shot ③
  • Full-bleed #D72638. White type only. Zero images. Story Template B, at full scale.
  • One statement at a time, Display XL, filling the screen:
    WE REFUSE SAFE CHOICES. → WE REFUSE FORGETTABLE WORK. → WE REFUSE TO BE JUST A VENDOR. → WE MAKE CULTURE.
  • Each enters in 200ms, holds, exits in 200ms. Between statements: one frame of black. Crimson → black → Crimson. Four frames of flicker across the whole scene — the most aggressive thing on the site, and it's over before it becomes a gimmick.

Reduced motion — a real design, not a switch

Under prefers-reduced-motion: the iris becomes a 400ms fade, cuts become 200ms fades, the whip pan becomes a straight cut, pinning releases into normal document flow, grain freezes at a single static frame, videos hold on poster frames with a visible play control. The site stays cinematic because darkness, scale and composition were carrying it — not the animation. That's the test of whether the direction is real.

E

Responsive Strategy

Mobile is designed, not derived. One section is mobile-first and adapted upward.

Desktop · 1280+

Horizontal negative space carries the composition. Custom cursor active (VIEW / PLAY / EXPLORE). Full scroll choreography — pinning, scrubbing, horizontal timeline. Vertical microdrama sits as an object in a void. Type runs past both edges.

Tablet · 768–1279

Pinning reduced to two scenes (reel, post). Horizontal timeline becomes a vertical one. Full-bleed frames survive intact. Cursor off, hover states become always-on at 60% opacity. Work metadata permanently visible.

Mobile · <768

Microdrama goes full-bleed and snap-scrolled — the phone is the frame, and this scene is better here than on desktop. Work frames stack full-bleed with permanent metadata (there is no hover on a phone; designing for it is designing for nobody). Nav is MENU → aperture overlay. Cursor off. One video decodes at a time.

Type on small screens

Display does not shrink proportionally. Display XL clamps at 44px minimum and still breaks the viewport edge. Body never drops below 16px. Labels stay 10px at +30% tracking. Touch targets ≥ 44px. Timid mobile type would undo the entire direction.

Performance — the constraint that keeps it premium

  • Poster-first, always. Every video renders as a graded poster frame; the source attaches only when an IntersectionObserver says it's within one viewport.
  • One video decoding at a time. Entering a scene pauses and detaches the previous one. Ten simultaneous videos is how cinematic sites become unusable on a mid-range Android.
  • Preload only the hero reel, and only its first ~3 seconds. Everything downstream is lazy.
  • Adaptive streaming (Mux or Cloudflare Stream) rather than self-hosted MP4s — a 40MB progressive file on a 4G connection in Bangalore defeats the entire premise. This is an open decision below.
  • Grain is one fixed layer, not per-element — a single composited SVG/canvas overlay on the GPU. Per-section grain would multiply paint cost for identical output.
  • Transforms and opacity only. content-visibility: auto on off-screen scenes. AVIF/WebP with explicit dimensions — zero layout shift, which also means zero jump in a scene that's supposed to be a held frame.
  • Budget: LCP < 2.5s, CLS < 0.05, hero payload under 1.2MB before video streams.

The showreel

One master cut, 16:9, graded. H.264 and WebM/AV1, at 1920 and 1280. A poster frame (the single best frame — your One Frame Rule). Plus: total runtime, and the seven chapter marks with titles for the scrub. If there are no chapters yet, give me the reel and I'll propose the marks.

Per project: title · client · category · year · runtime · credits, one hero film (16:9 or 9:16) + poster, 3–6 graded stills, 2–3 BTS stills, and two or three sentences of description. Not a case study — the film argues, the copy footnotes.

9:16, graded, with poster frames. Per episode: series title · episode number · one-line logline · runtime. If full episodes are too heavy, a 10–15s looping preview per episode plus a link out works and is faster.

Shot to your own photography direction: motivated hard light, mid-motion, eyes sharp, no forced smiles, signature grade applied. Ideally two per stage: idea, pre-production, production, post, final cut. Warm, candid, real. These carry Scene 06 and they're the section that makes the studio feel human rather than institutional.

Names, mono-white SVG logos if you have them, and written permission to display the work. I will not invent a client relationship or use a logo you can't show. 09

Copy + contact details

Confirm the real email, Instagram handle, and studio location line. Confirm the four manifesto statements. Everything else I'll write from the brand book's approved voice and send for approval — I won't invent claims about work you haven't done.

2 · Version stamp. The file is named v2.0 but the document footer and cover both read Version 1.0, and the roadmap lists "Brand book v2.0" as a Year 4 deliverable. Worth reconciling before this gets circulated — I've worked from the content, which is unambiguous either way.

Open Decisions

Three calls that change the build. I have a recommendation on each — say "go" and I'll take the defaults.

01

Video hosting

Recommended: Mux or Cloudflare Stream. Adaptive bitrate, automatic poster extraction, real analytics. Self-hosted MP4s in /public will work for a demo and will hurt on Indian mobile networks the moment the reel is over ~15MB.

02

The cold open, on every visit?

Recommended: once per session (sessionStorage), 600ms iris for returning visitors, skippable on any input always. A 3.2-second opening is a gift the first time and a toll booth the fourth.

03

Build order

Recommended: ship the full film in placeholder mode first — every scene, every transition, real motion, elegant placeholders — so you can feel it and direct it before a single asset is finalised. Assets then drop into content/ with no redesign. The alternative is waiting on the reel, and the reel will take longer than the site.
